📚 Section 12: Viva / Presentation Guide & Answers
1. Why was this project selected?
It provides a compelling interactive application of core Computer Graphics concepts (3D transformations, perspective projections, dynamic lighting, real-time collision detection, and procedural animation) within an educational disaster management and survival game.
2. Which Computer Graphics transformations are used?
• Translation [T]: Moving player, updating falling debris kinematics $\vec{p}(t) = \vec{p}_0 + \vec{v}t + \frac{1}{2}\vec{g}t^2$, and ground seismic wave displacement.
• Rotation [R]: Euler angle rotations for building swaying, falling debris tumbling, and 6-DOF camera roll/pitch during tremors.
• Scaling [S]: Pulsing beacon rings in the safe zone and dynamic UI warning animations.
3. How is earthquake shaking generated?
Through composite harmonic sinusoidal wave synthesis: $d(t) = A_1 \sin(\omega_1 t) + A_2 \sin(\omega_2 t + \phi)$. Ground displacement and rotational sway are applied to building matrices, with amplitude scaling linearly with height.
4. How is camera movement implemented?
Using spherical coordinate transformations (yaw and pitch) for mouse look and View Matrix calculations. For third-person, an offset vector is rotated by yaw. 6-DOF trauma-based shake offsets are added to the camera position and rotation.
5. How does collision detection work?
Axis-Aligned Bounding Box (AABB) overlap testing. If the player's bounding box intersects a building or fallen debris block, movement along that axis is zeroed, enabling smooth sliding collision response.
6. How are different earthquake intensities represented?
Via the Richter scale parameter (Level 1: 5.5 Mw, Level 2: 6.9 Mw, Level 3: 8.4 Mw), which directly scales wave amplitude $A$, oscillation frequency $\omega$, debris spawn density, camera trauma, and restricts escape time.
7. What are the game's win and lose conditions?
Win: Player reaches within the safe zone radius before the countdown timer expires. Lose: Timer reaches 0.0s before reaching safety.
8. Which parts are simulation and which parts are game mechanics?
Simulation: Harmonic wave ground displacement, building structural swaying, gravity kinematics of falling debris, and trauma camera shake.
Game Mechanics: Countdown timer, player health/stamina, minimap radar, scoring system, and obstacle route blocking.
